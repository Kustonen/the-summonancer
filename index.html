Cryptic Laboratory

“It's not a crypt! It's a Laboratory!”

Research for your skeletons, warriors and archers alike. Paid in gold, and in bones and skulls once things get serious.

Wisping Well

“Drop a coin in the well and you probably just lose a coin.”

“With all the spirits hovering about, you could actually lose a lot more than the coin - so if you drop one you better drop several...”

Wisps gather here between battles. Offerings make them brighter, steadier and a little less easily distracted by swamps.

Spirit Stones

A ring of humming stones around the well, where spirits rest between battles. Offerings here make them sturdier, quicker, harder to hit and, eventually, able to mend themselves.

The Summoning Septagram

“Pentagrams are for amateurs, Hexagrams are for nerds!”

Seven points, seven times the binding. New kinds of summons are bound here once, for good. After that they only cost mana and bones on the battlefield.

The Golematorium

“Some assembly required. Mostly rocks.”

“Also a disclaimer: While meth means death and adding E in front brings the unliving to life that does NOT mean it's necromancy!”

Where golems are chiselled, polished and occasionally reassembled. Research here makes them bigger, harder and much harder to ignore.

Saina's Wagon

“Every coin a verse, every kobold a chorus.”

Saina Lot keeps the ledger of everything you have done since your very first battle in this town. When the time comes to move on, this is the tally she will sing.

Spellbook

“Pack light. Pack spells.”

Choose which of your spells come along to the battlefield. Every spell takes a slot, Command and Nemesis included; the Spelltower can add more slots, for a price. Move and Shove don't need one.

Marketplace

“Everything has a price. Mostly a bad one.”

The merchant buys in bulk and sells by the piece, and somehow comes out ahead either way.

The Overdue Library

“Please return all spells by the due date. Fines are payable in souls.”

Every spell the wizard knows, plus a few nobody has returned in centuries. Study here makes Command and Nemesis last longer, cost less and hurt more.

Protective Stone Circle

“Stonehenge? We just keep our sheep penned here. They do tend to attract druids though...”

The ward draws its strength from these stones. Thicker stones take more hits, and the circle can learn to shove enemies away from the ward.

The Tavern

“Come for the ale, stay because the door sticks.”

Villagers, rumours and suspiciously cheap ale. If anyone in town needs something done, this is where they'll ask. Finished jobs are paid here too.

Blacksmith

“Strike while the iron is hot. Or lukewarm. I'm not fussy.”

Order what you need and the smith works through the list one job at a time, even while you're out on the battlefield. You can order things made from what earlier jobs will produce. Materials are taken one piece at a time as work starts; pausing or removing a job hands back whatever was on the anvil.

Invisible Academy

“Attendance is mandatory. Visibility is optional.”

Summons learn new skills here, once, and choose which ones they use. Every summon has one skill slot to start with; their own buildings can add more, at a price. Skills are improved in each summon's own building.

The Chrono Crystal

“Time flies when you're summoning. Now it flies twice as fast.”

A crystal that hums slightly faster than everything else around it. On every battlefield a Speed button now switches between normal and double speed: everything (enemies, summons, mana, spells, waves) happens twice as fast. The setting is remembered between battles.

Archivus Arcanus

“You are not prepared!”

Shelves of forbidden tomes on how to modify a summon until it is something else entirely. The archivist checks your library card, sniffs and shuts the door. Come back when you are more experienced.

The Grand Grimoire of Everything

Everything the wizard has learned about the things that try to reach the ward, and about the things he summons to stop them. Enemy pages fill in as you defeat more of that kind, ally pages as you summon more of them: first how it looks and walks, then how it fights, how it behaves and the embarrassing details. What an enemy carries is the last thing to learn: only after hundreds of them have fallen.

Warehouse

Everything carried back from the battlefield ends up here.

Field notes

Pick a summon below and tap a lit tile near the ward. The ward holds as long as nothing reaches it.

Summons

Wisp floats through anything. Alone it pokes at enemies; once other summons are around, it drifts to them and mends their bones instead. Wisps find swamps irresistible and sometimes wander off to admire one.

Skeleton walks around obstacles and hits hard. Archer shoots from two tiles away but is slow and fragile. Golem is slow, very tough and takes two summon slots: nearby enemies can't help attacking it, and every fourth blow is a ground slam that shoves them back. A Spirit drifts through anything and hovers right on top of enemies, hurting everything beneath it; badly hurt, it falls back to a wisp to mend. New summons are bound at the Summoning Septagram in town.

Spells

Command calls every summon toward a tile for a moment. Nemesis marks one enemy, and every summon goes after it. Once the Stone Circle has learned it, Shove pushes enemies away from the ward. A Guardstone keeps the first summons to step into its circle on guard there. Freeze chills an area and slows everything in it; Nurture grows bare earth into grass, grass into bushes and bushes into trees.

Assault

On the Assault field the enemies pour out of a red ward at the top. Summons with nothing to fight nearby march up and attack it. It is armoured (wisps barely scratch it; wards are built to keep spirits out), it pulses to hurt summons right next to it, and every third of its health lost it spits out a squad of defenders. Break it to win; spells that hit a tile can't touch it.

Gauntlet and Raid

In the Gauntlet enemies pour in through the side gates (a light warns which one). Defeat enough and the great gate opens to the next, harder cave; your summons, mana and loot come along and the ward recovers some strength. In the Raid the chief walks down the carpet once its guards are beaten. In both, Move shuffles the ward a tile a second; no summoning while it moves.

Enemies

Kobolds scatter whenever one of their own drops. Goblins lose their nerve when four fall in quick succession, unless an Orc is nearby to shout at them. Ogres (from wave 17) never panic and every sixth or seventh blow slams every summon around them. Trolls (from wave 20) mend themselves unless they burn, and fire hurts them twice as much. From wave 10, they arrive in tight packs, and from wave 40 every wave makes them a little tougher and harder-hitting.

Terrain

Sand and puddles slow everyone a little, swamp a lot. Bushes slow enemies only. Boulders, trees, lakes and rivers stop anyone on foot; cross rivers by the bridges.

Bones and skulls you collect here go to the warehouse in town when the battle ends.
